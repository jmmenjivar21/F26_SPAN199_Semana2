Polite words and clear instructions

Much of patient care is giving instructions: Pase. Siéntese. Respire profundo. Firme aquí. In this chapter you'll learn to give those instructions in Spanish with the formal (usted) command, make them negative (No se mueva), and soften them with courtesy expressions like por favor, con permiso, and disculpe. You'll also meet the informal (tú) command for talking to children, and practice choosing the right register for every person in the room.

Nota cultural · Courtesy in the clinic

Un mandato con cortesía

A bare command like Siéntese is grammatically correct, but on its own it can sound abrupt. Spanish speakers soften instructions with por favor, with the patient's title (Siéntese aquí, señora), or with a question: ¿Puede sentarse aquí, por favor? Before you enter a room, touch a patient, or pass in front of someone, say con permiso. And when a patient thanks you, de nada works everywhere, while con mucho gusto is very common in Central America and Colombia.

mandato + por favor con permiso ¿Puede…? con mucho gusto

¡OJO! · three kinds of "excuse me"

Con permiso asks permission before an action (entering a room, taking a pulse). Disculpe and perdón get someone's attention or apologize for a small inconvenience. Lo siento expresses real sympathy: Lo siento mucho, señora. Don't use lo siento to squeeze past someone in the hallway.

IRREGULARES · memorize these five

ir → vaya · ser → sea · estar → esté · dar → dé · saber → sepa. Example: Vaya a la farmacia. Spelling changes keep the sound: sacar → saque la lengua, llegar → llegue temprano, empezar → empiece.

G3Mandatos negativos y pronombres

For a negative usted command, just put no in front. The tricky part is where reflexive pronouns go.

VerboAfirmativoNegativoEnglish
comerComa.No coma nada.(Don't) eat.
tomarTome la medicina.No tome alcohol.(Don't) take / drink.
sentarseSiéntese.No se siente.(Don't) sit down.
levantarseLevántese.No se levante.(Don't) get up.
moverseMuévase.No se mueva.(Don't) move.
preocuparsePreocúpese.No se preocupe.(Don't) worry.
¡OJO! · pronoun placement

Affirmative: the pronoun attaches to the end, and you add a written accent to keep the stress: siente + se → Siéntese, acueste + se → Acuéstese. Negative: the pronoun goes before the verb, no extra accent: No se siente, No se mueva.

Short irregular tú commands: ven (come) · di (say) · haz (do) · pon (put) · ten (have) · ve (go) · sal (leave) · sé (be).

With a child: Abre la boca, mi amor. Muy bien. No te muevas.

¡OJO! · careful with look-alikes

Abre (tú command) and abra (usted command) differ by one vowel, and that vowel changes the register. With an adult patient, Abre la boca sounds too familiar. With a child, Abra la boca sounds oddly stiff.

LA CLAVE · softening

Three easy ways to sound kinder: add por favor, add a title (señora, don Luis), or turn the command into a question with ¿Puede…?: ¿Puede firmar aquí, por favor? In a pediatric visit you may use all three registers at once: tú with the child, usted with the mother, ustedes with the whole family.

En el consultorio dental

The dental chair is the perfect place to practice this week's grammar: almost everything a dentist says is a polite usted command. Abra la boca, por favor. Muerda suavemente. Enjuáguese. Learn the key words, listen to the dialogue, and check what you know at the end.

¡OJO! · muela vs. diente

El diente is any tooth, but patients usually call the back teeth las muelas: Me duele una muela. A wisdom tooth is la muela del juicio. And caries is the same in singular and plural: una caries, dos caries.

Los Algodones: "Molar City"

Los Algodones, México, conocido como “Molar City”, recibe a miles de pacientes de EE. UU. y Canadá que buscan tratamiento dental más económico. The small border town near Yuma, Arizona, has hundreds of dental offices, and many of its patients are retirees. Some of your future patients may have had dental work done there, so it's worth asking: ¿Tuvo algún tratamiento dental en otro país?
